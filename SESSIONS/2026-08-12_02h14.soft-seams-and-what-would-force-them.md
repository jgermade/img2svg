# Soft seams, and what would force them

Started 2026-08-12 02h14. Planned; nothing implemented yet.

Step 6 of [2026-08-11_22h08](2026-08-11_22h08.illustration-and-the-working-scale.md),
whose steps 1–5 shipped. It was left out on purpose there — the two images stopped
forcing it once the working scale removed the grain — and this file is what it
would take to build it, written after measuring, because the first three things I
said about it were wrong.

## What it is

A `<linearGradient>` where the source shades, a hard edge where the source has an
edge, decided by one property of the **source** and not of the palette:

> A boundary whose mixture zone is one pixel wide is an edge the source drew hard,
> and it stays hard. A boundary that mixes over many pixels is a soft transition,
> and a gradient across it is the honest rendering.

`ramp.rs` already merges a stack of bands into one gradient when one gradient
beats a flat colour at reproducing all of them. What it cannot do — by design, and
for a good reason — is the **two-band** case: it requires `MIN_COLORS = 3` distinct
palette colours, because any two adjacent regions can be explained by some shallow
ramp, and accepting them all would turn every drawing into mush. Softness is the
property that would let a pair through without opening that door.

## The measurements, and three corrections

Taken with `.tmp/scripts/mixwidth.py`: walk rows and columns, find runs where the
colour keeps changing, and record how wide each run is and how far the colour moved
across it. Fixed noise floor (0.01 in Oklab), because an adaptive one makes two
scales of the same image incomparable — removing grain lowers the floor and
lengthens every transition, which is the comparison being made.

Transition widths, in the pixels of each image:

| image | 1 px | 2 px | 3–4 | 5–8 | 9–16 | ≥17 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Sonic1.png` as it arrives, 1800×2823 | 17% | 21% | 23% | 26% | 9% | 0% |
| the same at its working scale, 383×600 | 0% | 9% | **41%** | **37%** | 10% | 0% |
| `cover.jpg` as it arrives, 300×300 | 4% | 4% | 17% | 30% | 28% | 14% |
| the synthetic grainy sky, 900×600 | **100%** | | | | | |

### Correction 1: resampling does not destroy the signal, it creates it

I said downscaling would narrow the mixture zone until a soft transition looked
hard — an 8 px ramp at ×0.21 being 1.7 px. Measured, the opposite happens: at the
working scale **nothing** is 1 px wide and 78% of transitions are 3–8 px, while the
colour jump across them roughly triples (0.06–0.09 in the source against 0.21–0.34
at the working scale). Removing the grain is what makes a wide ramp legible as one
continuous thing instead of a series of noisy steps. The signal is better at the
working scale, not worse.

### Correction 2: the threshold cannot be «one pixel»

That is what the criterion above says, and no real image agrees with it. The album
cover is flat poster art with black ink lines — every one of its edges *should* read
as hard — and only 8% of its transitions are 1–2 px wide, while 58% span 5–16.
A 300 px JPEG of a poster has no hard edges left in it: chroma subsampling and
ringing spread every one of them over several pixels.

So «one pixel» is not the boundary between hard and soft. Whatever the threshold
is, it has to be found from a distribution, and the cover says it is not universal.

### Correction 3: a generic edge measurement will not do

The numbers above cannot found a criterion, and it is worth being precise about
why: a run of consecutive change is not a transition. Across a two-pixel ink stroke
on skin the colour keeps moving for six or eight pixels — skin, into the stroke,
out of it — so the scan reports one wide «transition» that is really two hard edges
back to back. On a noisy JPEG that happens everywhere. That is why the cover
measures as soft when it is not.

The fix is available and it is what makes the criterion buildable: **the pipeline
knows the two colours**. A boundary in `Regions` separates region `A` from region
`B`, and their palette colours are known before anything is measured, so the
question stops being «where does colour change» and becomes «how many pixels along
this normal are a mixture of these two specific colours» — which is exactly the
projection `speckle::mixture` already computes for the fringe test. Same primitive,
third use.

## What it would take

### 6a — Softness per boundary

For each half-edge, sample the source perpendicular to the boundary at each of its
points, `±K` working pixels, and count the consecutive samples whose colour lies
within `MIX_CEILING × tolerance` of the segment between `A`'s and `B`'s colours.
The median of that count over the boundary is its **softness**, in working pixels.

- It runs after the labels are final and before `ramp::merge`, on the resampled
  image, which is the one whose pixels the geometry refers to.
- Cost is one pass over boundary points times `2K` samples. Boundary points are
  tens of thousands at the working scale, so this is milliseconds — but it is a new
  pass over image data and should be measured, not assumed.
- `K` has to be bigger than the widest zone worth calling soft; from the table
  above, 8 is a starting point at the working scale.

**Deliverable of this phase on its own:** the distribution of softness per boundary
on a real image, printed. Nothing emitted yet.

### 6b — The kill switch, and it is a real one

Look at that distribution before writing any gradient. The criterion exists only if
it is **bimodal**: a mode near 1–2 px that is the drawing's edges, and a mode at
several pixels that is its shading. If softness comes out unimodal — everything at
4–6 px, which is what the crude scan above measured — then there is no threshold
that separates a terminator from an ink line, and **step 6 should be abandoned
rather than tuned into looking right**.

This is written down because the previous attempt at this feature already made the
opposite mistake once. The first version of `ramp.rs` gave one gradient stop per
band, which is one parameter per data point: the model could not fail, and it
happily smeared two faces along a diagonal that did not exist. A criterion that
cannot be falsified is not a criterion, and the way to keep from building another
one is to decide in advance what result means «stop».

### 6c — What gets emitted, and the geometry is the hard part

A `<linearGradient>` expresses exactly one thing: colour as a function of position
projected on **one axis**. A stack of bands has one; a single soft seam does not
have to. Two cases, and they are not the same difficulty:

- **The seam is straight over its length** — a horizon, a lit wall, the edge of a
  panel. The union of the two regions carries a linear gradient along the seam
  normal, with two stops placed at the ends of the mixture zone. This is buildable
  with what exists: `Ramp` already holds its own rings, axis and stops, and
  `boundary::rings` already builds the union of a group of faces from the edges
  with one side inside it.
- **The seam curves** — the terminator on a sphere, the shading down Sonic's chest.
  There is no single axis, and forcing one is precisely the failure the last session
  hit. Three ways out: refuse it and keep the seam hard; fit a circle to the seam
  and emit a `radialGradient`; or cut the union into straight-ish runs, which
  invents new hard seams between the pieces and defeats the point.

Proposal: **6c does the straight case and refuses the curved one**, with the
refusal as a test rather than a limitation to apologise for. A curved terminator
smeared along an axis that does not exist looks worse than the banding it replaced.
The radial case is 6d, and only if an image demands it.

### 6d — Reconciling it with `gradient_step`, which is now in the way

Step 4 of the last session put `gradient_step` at 0.05 by default, and it is there
for the ink: a thin stroke is diluted ink, so the palette splits one stroke into two
tones. But what it does mechanically is **widen bands**, which enlarges the colour
step between them, and a large step is exactly what makes a seam look hard to any
gradient criterion. The two features pull against each other, and the fixture
already shows it: the synthetic ramp now comes out as a gradient *plus* one flat
band because the widened bands pushed the darkest one out of the group.

So step 6 has a prerequisite that step 4 deliberately skipped: **the ink merge has
to become targeted** — collapse the entries that are the same ink at different
dilution, and put `gradient_step` back to 0 for everything else. That was the
original step 4 as planned, and it was skipped because the generic knob measured
better on both images. It stops being enough as soon as 6 lands.

## The blocker, which is not code

None of the images at hand forces any of this:

- the cover is flat art; `ramp.rs` finds three gradients on it and they cost 2 KB;
- the grainy sky already comes out as **one** shape with one gradient, and its
  transitions measure 1 px 100% of the time — it is the case the existing detector
  was built for and it is solved;
- Sonic's shading now reads as deliberate cel-shading with clean edges. Whether
  that is wrong is a judgement about the drawing, not a measurement.

So the first thing this session needs is **an image where the shading looks banded
and should not** — ideally one that came up in real use, not one built to make the
feature fire. A synthetic case would be rigged by construction, and the last
session's lesson is exactly what rigging costs.

Until then, the order is: get the image, then 6a on it, then read the distribution
and either continue at 6b or write down that it was abandoned and why.

## What this is not

Not a fidelity project, same as before. A gradient here has to buy one of the four
properties — fewer colours, fewer curves, crisp edges where the source has them,
smooth shading where it shades — and «the output is closer to the photograph» is
not one of them. Two bands with a clean seam are a legitimate illustration of a
soft ramp; the bar for replacing them with a gradient is that the seam is visibly
wrong, not that it is visibly there.

## 2026-08-12 02h40 — The image arrived, and it refuses the phase plan above

Asked what image forces this, the answer was **Sonic's belly and muzzle**, with the
rest of the drawing called good. So the blocker is gone: `Sonic1.png` is the test
image and there are two named boundaries to measure instead of a guess.

### First, a mistake in how it was being looked at

Every "final" view of Sonic in the previous session was rendered into a 600×600
window on a drawing that is 383×600 at its working scale and 1800×2823 as it
arrives — **the bottom third was cropped off every time**. The shoes and the ground
shadow were never on screen while the results were being judged. Fixed with
`.tmp/scripts/shot2.sh`, which takes the height as a parameter instead of assuming
a square.

Two things were hiding down there, and one is a defect nobody had reported:

- the **ground shadow**, an airbrushed ellipse fading into white, comes out with a
  serrated edge — a saw-tooth where the source has a feather;
- the **shoe** has blotchy patches where the source has smooth highlights.

### And the two named boundaries are the case the plan proposed to refuse

Cropped at 3–4× against the working-scale source:

- **the belly** is a light peach oval with a smooth radial falloff, darker toward
  the rim. It comes out as a flat oval plus a **hard-edged darker crescent** along
  the lower-right, and the seam between them follows the curve of the oval;
- **the muzzle** is the same thing on a rounded snout: a light interior and a
  mid-tone band along the rim, with a hard seam that curves.

Which is exactly what section 6c proposed to **refuse**: "6c does the straight case
and refuses the curved one". That plan would have shipped a feature that leaves both
of the reported defects untouched. The curved case is not the optional extension —
**it is the case**, and the straight one is the easy corner of it.

### So the emission phase changes shape

A crescent whose seam follows a curve is not badly served by a gradient; it is badly
served by a **linear** one. What the belly wants is a `<radialGradient>`: a centre
near the highlight and stops going outward, which is what a rounded surface lit from
one side actually is.

The revised 6c: for a group whose seams are soft, fit **two** candidate models — one
linear along the best axis, one radial about a fitted centre — and keep whichever
reproduces the group's colours better, under the acceptance test `ramp.rs` already
has (beat a flat colour by `GAIN`, and stay within `CEILING` tolerances). A seam that
neither model explains stays hard, which is the same safety the current criterion
has, now with two models instead of one.

That makes the geometry work concrete rather than open:

- the linear axis already exists — `ramp.rs` takes the dominant eigenvector of the
  Oklab-on-position gradient matrix;
- the radial centre and radius come from the same moments: with colour as a function
  of distance from a centre, the centre is where that function is extremal, and a
  least-squares fit over the group's pixels gives it in closed form. The regions'
  moments (`area`, `Σx`, `Σy`, `Σx²`, `Σxy`, `Σy²`) are already computed for the
  linear fit.

`<radialGradient>` needs `fx`/`fy` only for an off-centre highlight; start with the
concentric form and add the focus if the belly asks for it.

### And a third case that is not this one

The ground shadow fades into **white by transparency**, not by colour: it is an
alpha ramp, and no colour gradient reproduces it. Either the gradient's stops carry
`stop-opacity`, which is a different code path in the emitter, or the shadow stays
as bands. Noted here so it does not get quietly folded into the same phase; it needs
its own decision.

Separately, and not part of this: a stray red speck survives at the corner of the
mouth, where the source has the mouth line. Worth one look at the speck filter, not
worth a phase.

## Revised order

1. **6a** softness per boundary, printed and nothing else — now with two boundaries
   whose answer is known in advance, which is what makes the measurement checkable:
   the belly and muzzle seams must come out soft and Sonic's ink edges must not.
2. **6b** the kill switch stands: if softness does not separate those two groups,
   the criterion does not exist and this gets abandoned rather than tuned.
3. **6c** two candidate models, linear and radial, under the existing acceptance
   test. The belly is the acceptance case; the flag test in `tests/illustration.rs`
   —four hard stripes that must stay hard— is the refusal case.
4. **6d** `gradient_step` back to 0 with a targeted ink merge in its place, because
   widening bands hardens seams and the two features fight.
5. the alpha ramp of the ground shadow, on its own, once 6c is settled.

## 2026-08-12 03h10 — Y un defecto nuevo, que es de lo que se entregó anoche

Reportado sobre el cover, con el dibujo grande delante: **manchitas claras en la piel
de la cara de abajo a la izquierda** y **un borde ocre alrededor de las letras**. Con
la sospecha, correcta, de que es cosa de la escala. (De Sonic, en cambio: «me gusta
mucho».)

### Qué es

El borde ocre es el halo del JPEG alrededor de las letras blancas, y las manchitas
son el mismo artefacto sobre la piel. A 300 px ese halo mide un píxel y lo absorben
los filtros; subido a 600 mide dos o tres y **es una entrada de paleta propia**:
`#bda59c`, un tono de piel desaturado que no existe en el dibujo y sí en el ringing.
De ahí que la paleta salga con tres tonos de piel donde el cartel tiene uno.

Medido, ninguno de los mandos actuales lo quita:

| qué se probó | resultado |
| --- | --- |
| `--smoothing 4` y `6` | 84,3 → 81,3 → 82,0 KB, y el borde sigue ahí |
| `--min-thickness` 4, 6, 9 | 79,8 → 78,4 KB, sigue ahí |
| techo de mezcla a 6 y a 12 | 74,3 → 71,4 KB, sigue ahí |
| `--no-simplify` | **desaparece** (y vuelven los trazos roscos y el óvalo octogonal) |
| subir con filtro triangular, sin lóbulos negativos | sigue ahí, así que **no es ringing del reescalado** |

El test de rebordes no lo coge porque el halo **no es una mezcla de sus dos vecinas**:
alrededor de una letra blanca sobre verde, el halo del JPEG es cálido, y ninguna
mezcla de blanco y verde es cálida. Y el umbral de grosor no lo coge porque no es una
banda delgada mal clasificada: es una región normal de una entrada que existe.

### Y lo que eso destapa: no hay denoise delante de la paleta

El proceso, cuando sube de escala, es `JPEG -> x2 -> paleta`. El reescalado no
limpia nada al subir —sólo al bajar, donde promediar es lo que quita el grano—, así
que los artefactos del original llegan a la paleta **agrandados y fieles**. Lo único
parecido a un denoise que hay es:

- `color_precision`, que recorta el último bit y nada más;
- `smooth::regularize`, que actúa sobre **etiquetas** y por tanto *después* de que la
  paleta haya decidido, y que además se mide en pasadas —una anchura absoluta en
  píxeles de trabajo—, así que es el mismo desajuste de escala que tenía
  `filter_speckle: 4`: dos pasadas estaban calibradas sobre la retícula del original
  y a x2 se quedan cortas.

### La propuesta

Un denoise **antes** del reescalado, al tamaño del original, y dirigido a lo que el
artefacto es de verdad. El halo del JPEG es un error de **croma**: la compresión
submuestrea el color a la mitad de resolución y conserva la luz, y eso es exactamente
lo que se ve —cálido donde la verdad va de blanco a verde, con la luz más o menos
bien—. Así que:

1. **suavizar croma y no luz**, en Oklab, con un radio del orden del bloque de croma
   del JPEG (2 px del original). Quita el halo y no toca ni un canto, porque los
   cantos viven en la luz;
2. **una mediana de 3x3** para las motas sueltas, que es lo clásico contra el ruido
   impulsivo y no mueve bordes rectos.

Las dos van delante de la paleta, que es donde hoy el ruido entra sin que nadie lo
mire, y las dos son al tamaño del original: **no tiene sentido agrandar lo que se va
a borrar**.

Riesgo a medir, no a suponer: un dibujo cuyo color *sí* cambia en dos píxeles —un
trazo de color saturado y fino— pierde saturación con lo primero. La comprobación es
la de siempre: el cover con las letras limpias y los trazos enteros a la vez, y
Sonic sin tocar, que es el que ya está bien.

## 2026-08-12 12h00 — 6c construido, y el modelo no podía elegirse al final

6c estaba «hecho» en el commit `wip: 6c`: `Model::{Linear, Radial}`, `region::Axis`,
`<radialGradient>` en el documento, y el modelo elegido **una vez por grupo, con el
grupo ya cerrado**, razonando que probar cuatro modelos en cada paso del crecimiento
multiplicaría la etapa entera. Sonic salía con dos degradados radiales y la barriga
caía en redondo, así que parecía terminado.

Lo que faltaba era el test de aceptación, y escribirlo destapó que estaba mal de
raíz. Un barrido de costuras del mismo par de colores, de arco cerrado a recta —una
imagen de 60x60, dos colores impuestos, la costura difuminada 10 px:

| costura | antes | qué pasaba |
| --- | --- | --- |
| r 20, centro dentro | **0 degradados** | el eje rechaza el grupo y el radial no llega a probarse |
| r 40, centro dentro | **0 degradados** | igual |
| r 40, centro en la esquina | 0 degradados | igual |
| r 60 | 1, lineal | |
| r 100 | 1, lineal | |
| r 180, casi recta | 1, lineal | |

El grupo lo forma el modelo con el que se prueba cada candidata. Con el eje solo, el
recorrido de una banda curva sobre cualquier eje es larguísimo, el error se dispara y
la pareja se rechaza **antes** de que exista un grupo al que ponerle un centro. O
sea: elegir al final sólo sirve para grupos que el eje ya aceptaba. Los dos
degradados radiales de Sonic eran eso, un refinamiento de dos grupos que ya pasaban;
el caso que 6c venía a resolver —una costura de verdad curvada— seguía saliendo a
cero.

### Y al meterlo dentro del crecimiento aparecieron dos errores más

El primero, en el **recorrido** de una banda. Con el eje se acota por fuera con el
octógono, y una cota holgada es el lado seguro. Con una distancia a un centro no vale
**ninguna** cota convexa: las bandas de un degradado radial son anillos, y cualquier
convexo que contenga un anillo contiene su centro, así que el mínimo sale 0 en vez del
radio interior, el degradado se evalúa en el color del centro y el error de todos los
anillos se va al máximo. Con la caja alineada —lo que había— un disco sombreado desde
un punto de dentro salía a cero degradados y no por casualidad.

El segundo, en dónde va una **parada**. Estaban en `model.at(centroide)`, que para
una proyección es la altura media y para una distancia no: para un anillo completo el
centroide **es** el centro, así que la parada del color de dentro aterrizaba encima de
la del de fuera. Medido en el caso de r 40: el eje se equivocaba 104 milésimas de
Oklab y el centro 103, o sea nada, cuando bien medido el centro se equivoca 78.

Las dos cosas piden medir sobre los píxeles, que es lo que no quería hacer por coste.

### El centro sale de la costura, no de los colores

Los centros candidatos eran los centroides de los colores extremos del grupo. Eso
falla en cuanto el grupo no llega al centro, porque el centroide de un arco está en el
arco. Lo que sí lo da es la **geometría**: la línea de nivel de un degradado radial es
un círculo, así que la costura entre dos de sus bandas es un arco, y un ajuste
algebraico de círculo (`x² + y² = ax + by + c`, sistema de 3x3) da el centro directo.
Una costura recta hace singular el sistema y no propone nada, que es justo lo que se
quiere: ésa la explica el eje.

Con eso, un candidato por grupo en vez de tres, y sacado de la propiedad que ya
distingue sombreado de canto —la costura blanda—, que es la puerta que limita a
quién se le prueba.

### El desempate va al eje

Con el recorrido bien medido, el radial gana **siempre**, porque un arco es de verdad
un arco. Lo que cambia es cuánto (error en milésimas de Oklab, mismo barrido):

| costura | eje | centro | se queda |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| r 20 | 119 | 69 | centro |
| r 40 | 104 | 78 | centro |
| r 60 | 86 | 75 | eje |
| r 100 | 83 | 77 | eje |
| r 180, casi recta | 81 | 78 | eje |

Sin margen, una costura con 300 px de radio de curvatura se lleva un `<radialGradient>`
centrado fuera del lienzo que dibuja exactamente lo mismo que un lineal. Con
`PREFER = 0.8` el radial se lo queda mientras la curvatura se note. Los seis casos del
barrido salen ahora a un degradado cada uno, y los tres primeros radiales.

### El coste, que había que pagar bien

Medir alturas exactas es una pasada por centro. Sobre la imagen entera:

| | pasadas | conversión |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sonic1.png | 46 | 0,13 → 0,15 s |
| cover.jpg | 33 | 0,09 → 0,14 s |

Un 55% más en la portada no se sostiene. Los píxeles agrupados por banda una sola vez
—orden de cuentas, `Vec<u32>` con offsets— y cada banda medida la primera vez que se
pregunta por ella lo devuelven a **0,13 y 0,09**, porque un grupo con costura blanda
son dos o tres bandas de las cuatrocientas del dibujo. En un cartel de color plano el
índice no llega a construirse.

### Y una cosa que se probó y se deshizo

`todas_blandas` decía en su nombre y en su comentario que pedía **todas** las costuras
internas blandas, y el código pedía **alguna**. Al arreglar el código para que dijera
la verdad del nombre, medido: Sonic con los mismos 21 degradados repartidos en **15
bandas más** (95 → 110 paths, 30,3 → 33,0 KB) y la portada con dos degradados menos,
sin que se vea nada mejor en ninguna de las dos. Una transición que en un trozo se
estreche hasta parecer canto sigue siendo la misma transición, así que lo que se
arregló fue el nombre: `alguna_blanda`. Del ruido no protege esa cuenta, sino
`earns_it` y `BOOTSTRAP`.

### Dos sospechosos descartados por el camino

- **No es `speckle` fabricándolo.** Con `--filter-speckle 0 --min-thickness 0` el
  reborde sale igual. La hipótesis era que el filtro fundía una franja que cruza
  varios fondos en la vecina que domina en el total, pintando piel junto al verde;

- **No es el umbral de grosor.** Barrido: `--min-thickness` 3, 5, 7, 9 y 12 da 440,

### Lo que sí es, con el perfil delante

Cruzando el canto de una letra blanca sobre el panel verde, en el original a 300 px
(fila 150, la caja que señala la región `#bda59c` de grosor 1,18):

Dos cosas a la vez, y la segunda es la que importa:

1. la transición mide **seis píxeles del original** —doce a la escala de trabajo,

2. y **cada uno de esos píxeles es una mezcla limpia de sus dos lados**: el hueco al

Verdosa en el original y ocre en la salida. Ahí está el defecto, y no está en la
anchura: está en que a esos píxeles **se les pinta con un color que no está en su

están cerca de algo que ya existe—, así que caen por el atajo de `min_color_share`:
la entrada más cercana dentro de `SNAP_CEILING * tolerance`. Y la más cercana en
Oklab a `(186,209,183)` no es un verde claro, que no existe en la paleta, sino

techo de 0,18—. El resultado es un píxel verdoso pintado de beige, alrededor de cada
letra y de todo lo demás que tenga un canto suave.

La comprobación es directa: con `--min-color-share 0`, que le da entrada a todo, el
reborde ocre **desaparece** y sale lo que debe —verde pálido sobre el verde, rosa
pálido sobre el rosa—. No sirve como arreglo, porque cuesta 71 colores, 784 paths y
110,2 KB contra 16, 440 y 84,3, pero deja el mecanismo señalado sin ninguna duda.

### La propuesta: el atajo puede cambiar la luz, no el tono

El atajo hoy sólo mira la distancia total, y por eso admite un salto de tono. Lo que
la paleta ya sabe hacer es separar los dos ejes: `Oklab::chroma_distance` existe desde
que `gradient_step` da holgura **en luz** a propósito. Aquí hace falta lo simétrico:

> una entrada puede absorber un color que no se ha ganado el sitio sólo si además le
> queda cerca **en tono**; si el único sitio cercano tiene otro tono, que funde
> entrada.

Con eso, `(186,209,183)` deja de poder irse a un beige —su distancia de croma a
`#debdb5` es 0,054, por encima de la tolerancia— y se va al blanco o funda entrada,
que en los dos casos está en su recta. Y el ruido que el atajo existía para absorber
—escalones de *ringing* pegados a un negro que ya existe— no se mueve, porque ése sí
comparte tono con quien lo absorbe.

Qué hay que medir para aceptarlo, y en qué orden:

1. la portada: el reborde tiene que irse **y** los colores no pueden dispararse. La
   referencia mala es `min_color_share: 0` con 71 colores; esto tiene que quedar
   mucho más cerca de 16 que de 71;
2. Sonic y el cielo sintético, que no tienen este problema: no deben moverse;
3. la tabla del corpus, porque el atajo es lo que sujeta la cuenta de entradas en
   imágenes grandes y aflojarlo es lo que la hacía crecer con el ruido.

**Interruptor:** si para quitar el reborde hay que subir los colores por encima de
unos veinticinco, no compensa y se queda como está. El cartel tiene sus tonos
contados y una paleta que se dispara es el defecto que `min_color_share` vino a
arreglar.
