# Better shapes, colours and gradients

Started 2026-08-11 02h10.

## What was reported

Two conversions were rejected, both in `examples/results-to-improve/`:

- `cover.jpg` → `cover.svg`. A flat-colour illustration (the *Blur* album cover):
  four colour blocks, four faces drawn in heavy black strokes. Verdict: *"debería
  reconocer los trazos y simplificar mejor las formas y colores"*.
- `Sonic1.png` → `Sonic1.svg`. An airbrushed illustration with soft shading.
  Verdict: *"no se han generado bien los degradados"*.

Both were produced with the defaults, which is the same thing the published page
does: `tolerance 0.045`, `color_precision 5`, `filter_speckle 4`,
`min_thickness 1.0`, `gradient_step 0` and — the one that shows first —
`--fit pixel`.

## What was measured

Tooling written for this, all under `.tmp/scripts/` and disposable:

| script | what it does |
| --- | --- |
| `svg2png.py` | rasterises a `--fit pixel` SVG exactly (every edge is axis-aligned on integers) |
| `regions.py` | area of every ring by the shoelace formula, bucketed |
| `colors.py` | how much of the canvas each palette entry actually paints |
| `grain.py` | decodes a PNG by hand and measures local colour noise in Oklab |
| `svgshot.sh` | renders any SVG through headless Chrome, to look at it |

### The output is mostly noise

`Sonic1.png`, current defaults — 12954 regions, 2.4 MB:

| region area | count | share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| under 16 px | 8911 | 68.8% |
| 16–64 px | 3131 | 24.2% |
| 64–256 px | 507 | 3.9% |
| over 256 px | 405 | 3.1% |

Median region area: **10 px**. Everything under 256 px paints **6.2% of the
canvas** and accounts for **97% of the paths** — so roughly 94% of that 2.4 MB
draws almost nothing. The drawing is some 400 shapes wearing 12500 specks.

`cover.jpg` is the same shape of problem at a smaller scale: 699 regions, of
which 469 (67.1%) are under 16 px.

### Raising the tolerance does not fix it, and neither does the speckle filter

| `Sonic1.png` run | regions | under 16 px | size |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| defaults | 12954 | 8911 (68.8%) | 2467 KB |
| `-t 0.08` | 5719 | 3851 (67.3%) | 1577 KB |
| `--filter-speckle 64` (16× default) | 1881 | 778 (41.4%) | 1336 KB |

The *proportion* of specks barely moves with tolerance: the noise floor is not
at a particular tolerance, it follows the tolerance wherever it goes.

### Why: the label field has no spatial term

`grain.py` over 24×24 windows of `Sonic1.png` that the eye reads as one flat
colour, distance in Oklab from each pixel to its own window's mean:

| window | median | p90 | max |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| blue, top of head | 0.0089 | **0.0320** | **0.0712** |
| blue, spikes | 0.0078 | 0.0219 | 0.0494 |
| white, face | 0.0038 | 0.0071 | 0.0130 |
| cream, muzzle | 0.0040 | 0.0082 | 0.0145 |

Against a tolerance of **0.045**. In the blue — a scanned airbrush, so it carries
film grain — one pixel in ten sits further from its neighbours' mean than the
whole tolerance, and the worst sit at 1.6× it.

`Palette::build` decides the entries globally and `Palette::lookup` then assigns
**every pixel independently** to the nearest one. Nothing in that pipeline knows
that a pixel has neighbours. So wherever the local grain reaches the tolerance —
which is every airbrushed, scanned or JPEG-compressed area, i.e. most of any real
image — adjacent pixels flip between two entries, and the connected-component
pass faithfully turns that flicker into thousands of islands and holes.

That is the root cause, and it explains all three complaints at once:

- **the specks**, directly;
- **the gradients**, because a ramp is where the image *lives* near a palette
  boundary for a long stretch, so the band edges come out as ragged noisy fronts
  instead of clean fronts — which is what "los degradados no se han generado
  bien" is looking at;
- **the strokes**, because a contour that wobbles pixel by pixel gives the curve
  fitter nothing to fit: it either chases the wobble or blows past its tolerance.

`speckle::filter` is a cleanup *after* the fact and cannot win — it deletes
specks one at a time while the assignment keeps making them.

### The palette founds entries nothing asked for

`cover.jpg`, current defaults: **65 entries**. Where the area goes:

- 8 entries paint 87% of the canvas, 13 paint 94%;
- **42 entries paint less than 0.2% each, and 1.45% between all of them.**

Those 42 are JPEG ringing around the black strokes. `Palette::build` walks the
distinct colours most-frequent-first and founds a new entry for anything that is
not within `tolerance` of an existing one — with no floor on how rare that colour
may be. Frequency orders the seeding but never gates it, so a colour that occurs
thirty times in the whole image founds an entry as readily as the background
does. Greedy first-fit also never revisits: an entry sits wherever the colour
that happened to seed it sat, not at the centre of what it ended up representing,
and two entries can finish closer to each other than `tolerance`.

### Nothing ever simplified a shape

Both rejected files carry `shape-rendering="crispEdges"`: they are `--fit pixel`,
the literal staircase of pixel edges. That is the CLI default (`FitArg::Pixel`)
and the page's default (`web/views/modes.jsx:104`). No strokes were recognised
and no shapes were simplified because on this path nothing ever tries to. The
fitters exist and work; they are just not what anyone gets.

## The plan

Four phases. The order is forced: each one needs the previous to be worth doing.

### Phase 1 — quiet the label field

The fix for the root cause, and what unblocks the rest. Regularise the labels
**spatially** before boundaries are extracted.

Add a majority-vote pass over the label map: each pixel takes the most frequent
label among itself and its 8 neighbours, iterated a small number of times, in
`cluster.rs` between the run labelling and `speckle::filter`. Working on labels
rather than on colours means it cannot invent a colour that is not already in the
palette, and it leaves genuine edges alone — an edge has a clear majority on each
side, so only isolated flips move.

New option `ClusterOptions::smoothing: usize` (passes; `0` keeps today's
behaviour), reachable from CLI and page.

Considered and rejected as the *primary* fix: pre-blurring the image. It is one
pass and cheap, but it moves real edges as well as noise, and it can produce
colours that are in no palette entry. Worth revisiting as a complement if the
majority vote alone does not carry the airbrushed case.

Expected: the sub-16 px population collapses, since those regions exist only
because of single-pixel flips. To be measured, not assumed.

### Phase 2 — a palette that reduces

Three changes to `Palette::build`, smallest first:

1. **A floor on founding an entry.** A colour outside `tolerance` of everything
   still joins the nearest entry if it accounts for less than some fraction of
   the visible pixels. This alone should take most of the 42 hangers-on out of
   `cover.jpg`. New option; the fraction needs picking by measurement.
2. **A merge pass after building.** Entries that finished within `tolerance` of
   each other collapse, the survivor weighted by pixel counts. Greedy first-fit
   does not guarantee separation and today nobody checks.
3. **Lloyd refinement**, two to four iterations: move each entry to the centroid
   (in Oklab, weighted by pixel count) of the colours assigned to it, reassign,
   repeat. Entries then sit at the centre of what they represent instead of on
   whichever colour seeded them, which buys fidelity per entry — i.e. fewer
   entries for the same result.

Also give `max_colors` a non-zero default, once 1–3 make the number mean
something.

### Phase 3 — defaults that recognise strokes

- `photo` defaults to `--fit polygon` instead of `pixel`, on the CLI and on the
  page. `pixelart` keeps `pixel`, which is genuinely what a sprite wants.
- Re-tune `tolerance`, `filter_speckle` and `min_thickness` on top of phases 1–2,
  since all three currently carry the weight of compensating for the noise and
  should be able to relax once it is gone.

### Phase 4 — gradients as gradients

Separate and larger; proposed, not committed to in this session. Bands are the
best a flat-fill tracer can do, and phase 1 makes them clean bands instead of
ragged ones — but a ramp in an SVG should be a `<linearGradient>`. Detecting
regions whose colour varies monotonically along an axis and emitting one gradient
in place of N bands is its own piece of work, with its own decisions about when a
group of bands is a ramp.

## Tests

`tests/photo.rs`, `tests/cluster.rs`, `tests/speckle.rs` and `tests/corpus.rs`
all pin behaviour that phases 1–3 move on purpose. The snapshots get re-recorded
as each phase lands, with the session saying which numbers moved and why — not
re-recorded in bulk at the end, where a real regression could hide in the noise.
The synthetic drawing in `tests/photo.rs` already carries a gradient, two flat
blocks, a one-pixel line and loose dots, which is the right fixture for phase 1:
the flat blocks and the gradient must improve, and the one-pixel line and the
dots must not quietly disappear.

## Status: phase 1 done, phases 2–4 pending

Measurement scripts live in `.tmp/scripts/` and the experiment outputs in
`.tmp/exp/`; neither is part of the build.

## 2026-08-11 03h05 — Phase 1 landed: spatial regularisation

Approved with *"ok, dale caña"*. Phase 1 is implemented; phases 2, 3 and 4 are
untouched.

### What was built

**`src/smooth.rs`**, new module. Each pixel takes the palette entry that
minimises `distance(pixel colour, c) + beta × neighbours that are not c` — one
ICM step over a Markov field — iterated `smoothing` times, out of place so the
result does not depend on the scan order.

A plain majority vote was written up and rejected before coding it: a one-pixel
line is outvoted three to six inside its own 3×3 however black it is, and the
stroke-heavy `cover.jpg` is the case that has to work. The vote only counts
neighbours; what it is missing is how much the pixel *resembles* the colour being
proposed, and that second term is exactly what separates a grain pixel — halfway
between two entries, which is why it flipped — from a pixel of ink.

Three things fell out of the implementation that were not in the plan:

1. **The tolerance guarantee.** `tests/cluster.rs` pins
   *no pixel is painted further than `tolerance` from its colour*, and moving
   pixels for geometric reasons breaks it. Rather than weaken the promise, the
   step got a ceiling: a pixel may only land on an entry within `CEILING` × what
   it already accepted. Measured where to put it, on `Sonic1.png` at two passes:

   | ceiling | subpaths |
   | --- | --- |
   | 1× tolerance | 23,804 |
   | 1.5× | 17,468 |
   | **2×** | **16,959** |
   | none | 16,957 |

   At 2× there is nothing left to win, so the bound is free and it stays. At 1×
   it costs half the fix, because a flickering pixel sits *on* the decision
   boundary and its distance to the other entry is the tolerance and a hair more
   — cutting at the tolerance exactly forbids the very case being fixed.

2. **Straight boundaries were being serrated.** The first version turned the
   synthetic gradient in `tests/photo.rs` from 10 paths into 12, with the band
   edges in sawtooth. Cause: the palette assigns each colour to the nearest entry
   *of the ones that already existed*, not the nearest of all, so along any
   straight boundary there is a row of pixels that marginally prefer the entry
   opposite; with the neighbour counts tied, the colour term moved them. Fixed by
   requiring a candidate to leave the pixel agreeing with **more** neighbours
   than it does now. Improving colour accuracy is not this stage's job. After
   that, all four photo snapshots pass **unchanged** — on a clean drawing the
   step correctly does nothing, which is better evidence than a re-recording
   would have been.

3. **Guarantees restated where they are made.** `cluster.rs` said the bound
   flatly; it now names the stages that spend it and what each costs
   (regularisation: `CEILING` × tolerance and only toward a colour already
   painted adjacent; speckle: no bound). `tests/cluster.rs::opciones()` turns
   `smoothing` off alongside `filter_speckle` and `min_thickness`, which is the
   convention that was already there for exactly this reason.

### Measured

`Sonic1.png`, 1800×2823:

| passes | regions | subpaths | SVG |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 12,954 | 41,548 | 2,468 KB |
| 1 | 12,148 | 22,267 | 1,754 KB |
| **2 (new default)** | **10,151** | **17,003** | **1,468 KB** |
| 4 | 8,200 | 12,971 | 1,246 KB |

Subpaths down 59% at the default. It also **pays for itself**: 0.79 s end to end
against 0.84 s with it off, because the boundary and document stages have that
much less to chew.

Composing with the filter it does not replace — regularisation flattens flicker,
speckle removes compact blobs, which are a local minimum of the criterion and
only erode a ring per pass:

| run | regions | subpaths | SVG |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| speckle 64 alone | 1,881 | 14,405 | 1,336 KB |
| smoothing 2 + speckle 32 | 1,974 | 3,397 | 871 KB |

Region sizes at smoothing 2 + speckle 32: regions under 16 px fall from 68.8% of
the total (8,911) to 5.3% (108), median area 10 px → 67 px.

`cover.jpg` barely moves — 699 → 659 regions, 65 → 68 colours — and that is the
expected answer, not a disappointment: its problem is the palette founding 65
entries where 42 paint 1.45% between them, which is phase 2. What matters here is
that the strokes, glasses and hair survive at 4 passes, checked by eye at 1:1.
