## Where things stand

Built, all behind the `photo` cargo feature, which `cli` pulls in and `wasm`
deliberately does not:

| Piece | What it does | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `color::Oklab` | perceptual distance for the photo path | — |
| `Rgba::quantize` | `2^bits` levels per channel, nearest not truncated | — |
| `cluster.rs` | quantise → Oklab palette → components over runs | 340 ms at 4.2 Mpx |
| `boundary.rs` | cracks, nodes and chains → half-edge IR with `right` | 112 ms at 4.2 Mpx |
| `speckle.rs` | merges specks by area *and* thickness, on the labels | 9.6× fewer regions |
| `gradient_step` | widens bands along lightness only, off by default | 74 → 31 colours |
| `max_colors`, `palette` | cap or impose the palette | — |
| `background::remove_clustered` | drops the border-touching background, then crops | — |

**None of it is reachable yet.** `Segmentation` still has one variant, the CLI has
one subcommand, and the web knows nothing about any of this. The photo path is
library-only: `cluster::from_image` then `boundary::from_clustering` gives a
`Regions` that `svg::render` already accepts with `Fit::Pixel`, and that is the
whole of it — 480 ms end to end at 4.2 Mpx, SVG included.

## What was done, and what it changed about the plan

Four chunks of work — colour groundwork, clustering, boundaries, speck filtering —
60 new tests, and the eleven pixel-art snapshots unmoved throughout. Worth reading
before picking up the list below, because four of these corrected the plan rather
than following it.

### The `photo` feature

`photo = []`, pulled in by `cli` and deliberately not by `wasm`. Verified with
`cargo check --no-default-features --features wasm` — the exact invocation the web
build uses — so the split is a fact and not an intention. Declared early, with
almost nothing behind it, because dividing modules already written is the
expensive way to do it.

### Oklab, and a justification that was wrong

`color::Oklab`, pinned to Ottosson's published sRGB values as a test rather than
eyeballed. The plan's stated reason for it — that equal numeric distances in green
and blue are not equally visible, so one threshold shatters vegetation and smears
sky — was measured and turned out to be both backwards in its example and
overstated at ~1.27×. The real defect is sharper: those weights are luminance
coefficients, so the metric tracks *brightness change*, and on saturated colours
it **inverts** the eye's ordering. A full channel of blue pushed into saturated
yellow scores 19.9; a dark blue visibly shifting hue scores 13.3; Oklab puts the
second nearly six times further apart. No single threshold exists on the first
metric.

Also measured, and worth not overselling: on a greyscale ramp the RGB distance is
already decent — sRGB's gamma is roughly perceptual on its own — and Oklab corrects
shadows against highlights by only about 50%. The win is chroma, not light.

### Quantisation that does not darken the image

`Rgba::quantize` reduces each channel to `2^bits` levels and returns the
**nearest** one. The plan said to keep the top bits, as VTracer's
`--color-precision` does. Truncation always rounds down: half a level lost per
channel on average, four values out of 255 at 5 bits, and the whole image comes
out slightly duller. A test written to allow half a level of error is what
surfaced it, on `6 → 0` at 5 bits. The bias is invisible in any one colour and
plain across an image, so its test checks the mean over the ramp.

### Clustering, with the palette fixed first

`cluster.rs`: quantise, build a palette by grouping the distinct colours greedily
in Oklab from most frequent to least, then label connected components of equal
palette entry over runs with union-find.

The pseudocode in the plan said something else — cut runs against the previous
pixel, merge runs whose colours are within a threshold — and that was not built,
on purpose. Every such merge moves the group's colour, so down a smooth ramp the
chain of merges swallows the whole sky, which emerges as one flat region matching
neither of its ends, and the result depends on where the sweep started. Deciding
the palette first buys a bound worth stating: **no pixel is painted further than
`tolerance` from its own colour**, by construction. It is a test, not a claim. It
also removes all distance arithmetic from the inner loop, which is now integer
comparison.

8-connected, matching `trace::components`, for the cost of widening the overlap
test by one column. Emission order is colour-major because `svg::render` walks
contiguous runs of equal colour — get that wrong and every region gets its own
`<g>`.

### Boundaries, shared once

`boundary.rs`: over the lattice of pixel corners, a *crack* is a unit segment
between adjacent corners and a boundary where the labels differ, with outside and
transparency counting as one more label so the image border falls out for free.
Corners where three or four regions meet are nodes; a chain of cracks between two
nodes is exactly one half-edge, and `right` is populated — the thing phase 1
deferred and phase 4's entire seam story depends on.

It rests on a small lemma: at a corner with exactly two boundary cracks, both
separate the *same* pair of regions, straight through or turning. A
`debug_assert` re-checks it crack by crack instead of trusting the proof, and has
held across ~285,000 chains.

**A real topology bug, caught by a test.** Two pixels of one region touching only
at a corner produce two correct closed chains, and the first ring assembly glued
them into a **figure-eight**: both start and end at the shared corner, and it kept
chaining while anything was unused. A ring closes when it returns to its own
starting point, not when candidates run out. Even-odd fill would have painted the
figure-eight correctly, so this would have stayed hidden until a curve fitter
tried to close the chain.

### Two things wrong in the IR, invisible until something other than the grid used it

- **`ring_points` contradicted its own documentation.** It promises not to repeat
  the first point at the end, and with more than one half-edge it did, because the
  closing edge ends where the first began. Nothing had hit it: until now every ring
  was a single edge. Fixed in that one place rather than leaving each fitter to
  remember.
- **A closed half-edge now repeats its first point at the end**, and that
  repetition *is* the signal that the chain closes on itself — precisely what a
  curve fitter needs to treat it as periodic. `segment.rs` was changed to follow
  the same convention so there is only one of them, and the snapshots confirmed
  the drawing did not change.

### Speck filtering, with a criterion the plan did not have

`speckle.rs`, and the detail is in section 1 below since that is where it was
planned. The short of it: an area threshold — all that `--filter-speckle` is in
VTracer, and all the plan asked for — leaves nearly half its survivors as
one-pixel-wide antialiasing bands, because a 1×8 band has eight pixels. Adding a
thickness criterion, `2 × area / perimeter`, takes the same image from 4,157
regions to 1,298. It merges into the neighbour sharing the most border rather than
the biggest one, and it runs on the labels rather than on the IR.

Found by rasterising a conversion and looking at it, after the region counts had
been collected and had not shown it.

### What the tests lean on

The load-bearing check for boundaries is geometric: the **signed** area of a
region's rings must equal exactly `-area`. The absolute value alone already
forbids a missing or duplicated chain; the sign pins the orientation, with `y`
downward and the interior on the left giving negative area; and summing rather
than summing absolute values forces holes to subtract. It runs over the ~81,000
regions of a 1.4 Mpx noisy image, in debug, with the crack-level assertions live.

## 1. Speckle filtering — **done**, `speckle.rs`

At the default tolerance a real image gives 18k–31k regions and **68–77% of them
are specks of ≤4 px**. This is not a refinement; it is what makes the output
usable. Three measurements shape how to do it:

- **An area threshold alone leaves half the survivors behind.** Rasterising a
  conversion and looking at it shows two different kinds of speck: isolated dots,
  and **bands one pixel wide** running along every colour boundary, which are the
  antialiasing fringe of the source. A 1×8 band has eight pixels, so an area
  threshold — what the plan says, and what VTracer's `filter_speckle` does — does
  not touch it. Counted on the corpus: 68% of regions are ≤4 px, 78–85% are one
  pixel wide or tall, and **15–20% are thin *and* bigger than 4 px**. Filter on
  area alone at ≤4 and you go from 12,498 regions to ~4,100, of which ~1,900 —
  nearly half — are fringe bands. So the criterion wants thinness as well as area.
  Careful with a blanket rule against thin regions, though: in a photo a
  one-pixel-wide region is essentially always noise, but a deliberate thin
  highlight is not, so this is a knob to *look* at rather than to reason about.

- **Raising the tolerance does not substitute for it.** Past about 0.08 the region
  count climbs again — 9,409 regions at 0.08 against 11,690 at 0.15 on one image —
  because with few palette entries the pixels along a band boundary alternate
  between two distant representatives and shatter. Fewer colours is not fewer
  regions.
- **The plan's other prediction does not reproduce.** Unfiltered specks were
  supposed to make the SVG bigger than the PNG; measured it is 0.2–0.6× of it. The
  corpus PNGs are pathologically noisy pixel art (64k–159k distinct colours) and
  compress badly, which is why. The case rests on path count — 18,317 paths in one
  document — not on bytes.

### Where it went

**On the label image, before boundary extraction** — as reasoned below, and it
held up. Merging afterwards means
surgery on the IR: dissolving a half-edge, then re-chaining the rings of both
faces, with the interesting case being a speck whose removal joins two rings of
its neighbour into one. Doing it earlier is the same result for none of that:
relabel each small component to its largest neighbour, then extract boundaries
once, from labels that are already final.

The neighbour a speck should merge into can be found without building the IR:
one pass over the cracks, accumulating shared border length per pair of labels.
Largest *neighbour* by area is what VTracer does; shared border length is the
other reasonable choice and probably the better one for thin specks.

Do it by ascending area with union-find, so a speck whose only neighbours are
also specks lands somewhere sensible instead of needing repeated passes. Merging
only ever grows the survivor, so one ordered pass suffices — worth asserting that
no region under the threshold survives.

### What it measured, and how it was judged

| filter | regions | colours | SVG |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| none | 12,498 | 142 | 549 KB |
| area ≤ 4 only | 4,157 | 117 | 246 KB |
| **default: area ≤ 4, thickness < 1** | **1,298** | 74 | 117 KB |
| area ≤ 8, thickness < 1.5 | 787 | 58 | 72 KB |

The thickness criterion does more than the area one it was added beside: 4,157
regions become 1,298. Nine and a half times fewer paths than unfiltered.

The unit tests prove no speck survives, that no pixel is created or destroyed, and
that the emission order still groups colours; they cannot say whether the picture
still looks like the picture. That needed the eye, and not the CLI: write the SVG
from a test, screenshot it with headless Chrome, crop and upscale the interesting
corner with the `image` crate. All four settings above came out faithful, and the
thin black outlines of the eye and mouth — the deliberate thin features that the
thickness rule was the risk to — survive in every one of them.

## 2. The rest of the photo segmentation — **done**

- **`gradient_step`.** Done, and it relaxes the merge along lightness rather than
  quantising `l` outright — which avoids needing an Oklab→sRGB inverse and keeps
  every emitted colour one that exists in the image. Measured: 74 colours to 31 at
  0.15. The caveat is worth carrying forward, because it is the opposite of what
  the plan implied: on shaded artwork this *flattens the shading*, and past ~0.15
  it mottles the band boundaries. It is the tool for a smooth sky. Default 0.
- **Background.** Done, but not the way the plan said. It asked for a colour
  tolerance because exact comparison "never matches on a photo" — and it does
  match, because both paths unify near-equal colours upstream (`reduce_palette`
  and the palette respectively). The actual gap was that the photo path had no
  background removal at all, and on a label image it needs no flood fill either:
  the background is the set of regions touching the border. Enclosed same-colour
  areas stay for free, by definition rather than by traversal.
  Specks are filtered first, deliberately — see the module docs.
- **`max_colors` and a fixed palette.** Done, both inside `Palette::build`. Both
  drop the within-`tolerance` guarantee by construction, which is what asking for
  them means.

## 3. Wiring it up, and the one type that has to change

The refactor cost here is small but it touches the public surface, so it wants
deciding rather than discovering:

- **`Segmentation::Cluster(ClusterOptions)`.** Adding the variant breaks three
  irrefutable `let Segmentation::Grid(options) = …` patterns: `Config::grid_options`,
  `Config::grid_options_mut` and `convert_image` in `lib.rs`. They become matches.
- **`Conversion` does not fit a photo.** `grid`, `cell` and `offset` are
  meaningless there, and the phase-0 notes already ruled that turning them into
  `Option` is not acceptable: their type is in the `.d.ts` and the page reads
  them. So photo mode returns its own result type. What it should carry:
  colours, regions, paths, subpaths, and the background if one was removed.
- **CLI `img2svg photo`.** The subcommand shape is already in place for
  `pixelart`; this is its sibling, with `--tolerance`, `--color-precision`,
  `--filter-speckle`, `--gradient-step` alongside the shared flags.
- **wasm.** `read_config` maps flat option keys onto the config; the photo path
  needs its own reader and its own result binding, and the worker needs to know
  which one to call.

## 4. Phase 4: the fitters

What the IR now guarantees, and what each fitter may therefore assume:

- Every boundary appears **once**. Fit a half-edge, and both faces reference the
  same fitted geometry — one forward, one reversed. There is no seam to hide and
  no stacking needed. This is the thing VTracer cannot do.
- `points` is the **dense** polyline, one point per lattice corner.
- A chain that closes on itself **repeats its first point at the end**. That is
  the signal to fit it as periodic rather than leaving two loose ends.
- A chain's endpoints are nodes, shared with the chains that continue past them.

### `polygon`

Ramer–Douglas–Peucker per half-edge, tolerance in pixels. RDP keeps the endpoints
fixed, which is exactly what is needed: the nodes must not move, or the chains
meeting there would come apart. Nearly free, and the right answer for flat-shaded
art.

### `spline`

Corner detection over a turning-angle window, tangent estimation, least-squares
cubic fit with recursive subdivision at the worst point (Schneider). `path_data`
in `svg.rs` only knows `h`/`v` and needs a curve-aware sibling.

One thing the IR does *not* give for free: **tangent continuity across a node.**
Each chain is fitted independently, so two chains meeting at a node agree on the
point but not on the direction. For a three-region junction that is correct — a
real corner. Where it will show is a node that is geometrically smooth, and the
fix, if it turns out to matter, is to fit the tangent at shared nodes once and
hand it to both chains as a constraint. Worth knowing before it looks like a bug.

## 5. Phase 5: the web

- **Measure the wasm with `--features photo` first.** The baseline is 152 KB, and
  the rule of thumb was: under ~50 KB of growth, one bundle; over that, build two
  `pkg/` directories with `wasm-pack --features`. The feature gate exists and is
  verified to compile without `photo`, so this is now purely a measurement.
- **One entry in the `MODES` map** in `web/app.js`, plus the curves settings panel
  replacing the "no engine yet" notice.
- **Real progress.** Clustering and fitting are loops over rows and regions, so
  the photo path can report actual progress through a callback into JS instead of
  the indeterminate bar the sprite path uses. At half a second per image it
  matters more than it did at 150 ms.

## 6. Leftovers not tied to a phase

- **No goldens for the photo path.** The pixel-art safety net exists; the photo
  path has 30 unit tests and no snapshot. It cannot use the corpus, which is not
  in the repo, so it needs a synthetic fixture generated in-test the way
  `tests/golden.rs` builds its sprite — a gradient plus a couple of flat blocks
  would exercise palette, banding and speck filtering at once. Add it when the CLI
  can emit a photo conversion.
- **Corpus in CI.** Still not reachable from a clean clone; `REQUIRE_CORPUS=1` is
  wired and waiting. Release asset, fixtures repo, or LFS.
- **`.d.ts` guard.** Nothing catches a breaking change to the JS API. More
  pressing once photo mode adds a second result type.
- **`checker.rs` has no golden from a clean clone**, for the same corpus reason.
- **Homebrew Rust shadows rustup locally** — fix on the machine, not in the repo.
