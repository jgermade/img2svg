## Where things stand

The photo half of the segmentation axis is **done and tested, and reachable from
nothing**. 111 tests green in debug and release, the eleven pixel-art snapshots
never moved.

| Module | What it does | Cost at 4.2 Mpx |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `color::Oklab`, `Rgba::quantize` | perceptual distance, channel reduction | — |
| `cluster.rs` | quantise → Oklab palette → components over runs | 340 ms |
| `speckle.rs` | merges specks by area *and* thickness, on the labels | included |
| `boundary.rs` | cracks, nodes, chains → half-edge IR with `right` | 112 ms |
| `background::remove_clustered` | drops border-touching background, then crops | — |

Also in `ClusterOptions`: `gradient_step` (widen bands along lightness only,
default off), `max_colors`, a fixed `palette`, `remove_background`.

The whole photo pipeline today is two calls — `cluster::from_image` then
`boundary::from_clustering` — giving a `Regions` that `svg::render` already
accepts with `Fit::Pixel`. About 480 ms end to end at 4.2 Mpx including writing
the SVG. Everything below is either plumbing that exposes it, or the second axis.

## 1. Wiring, which is what blocks everything else

Nothing here is hard. It is listed in this much detail because it touches the
public surface, and that wants deciding rather than discovering.

### `Segmentation::Cluster(ClusterOptions)`

Adding the variant breaks three irrefutable patterns, all in `lib.rs`:
`Config::grid_options` (line 79), `Config::grid_options_mut` (84), and
`convert_image` (231). They become matches. `Config::grid` (72) and the `Default`
impl (99) construct rather than destructure and are fine.

### The result type, and why phase 0's constraint does not actually bind

`Conversion` carries `grid`, `cell`, `offset` and `checkerboard`, none of which
mean anything for a photo. The phase-0 notes ruled out making them `Option`
because "their type is in the `.d.ts` and the page reads them".

**That constraint applies to the wasm wrapper, not to the Rust type.**
`wasm::Conversion` (`wasm.rs:12`) is a separate `#[wasm_bindgen]` struct holding
`inner: crate::Conversion` with hand-written getters — `gridWidth` reads
`inner.grid.0` and so on. So the Rust type is free to change shape as long as the
wrapper compensates, and photo mode can get its own wrapper with its own getters,
which *adds* a type to the `.d.ts` instead of changing one.

Recommended shape:

```rust
pub struct Conversion {
    pub svg: String,
    pub colors: usize,
    pub paths: usize,
    pub subpaths: usize,
    pub background: Option<Rgba>,
    /// Lo propio de cada segmentación.
    pub detail: Detail,
}

pub enum Detail {
    Grid { grid: (usize, usize), cell: (f64, f64), offset: (f64, f64),
           checkerboard: Option<checker::Checkerboard> },
    Cluster { canvas: (usize, usize), regions: usize },
}
```

`convert_image` then dispatches on `Segmentation` and returns one type, which is
what keeps the three public entry points — `convert`, `convert_rgba`,
`convert_image` — at their current signatures.

**Keep accessors for the fields that move.** `grid`, `cell` and `offset` are read
by the doctest (`lib.rs:19`) and by five test files: `golden.rs`, `corpus.rs`,
`grid.rs`, `checker.rs` and `common/mod.rs`. Adding `grid()`, `cell()` and
`offset()` returning `Option<…>` keeps every one of those sites a one-word edit
instead of a `match`. That is not only about churn: `common/mod.rs` is what writes
the snapshot headers, so a careless rewrite there is the one way this refactor
could move the eleven snapshots.

`canvas` is worth carrying for photo mode because `remove_background` crops, so
the output size is not the input size.

### The CLI

`img2svg photo <INPUT>`, sibling to `pixelart`, sharing the flattened `-o`, `-q`,
`-b`, `--fit`. Its own flags map one-to-one onto `ClusterOptions`: `--tolerance`,
`--color-precision`, `--filter-speckle`, `--min-thickness`, `--gradient-step`,
`--max-colors`, `--remove-background`. A `--palette` would need a parser for a
list of hex colours; leave it to the library until someone asks.

Two things to get right, both learned the hard way in this pipeline:

- **`--min-thickness` is the one nobody else has.** Its help text should say what
  it is for, because "thickness" on its own invites setting it to 5.
- **`--gradient-step` flattens shading.** Say so in the help. It is the tool for a
  smooth sky and the wrong tool for a drawing with volume.

### wasm and the web

- `read_config` maps flat option keys onto the config; photo mode needs its own
  reader, its own result wrapper, and the worker needs to know which to call.
- One entry in the `MODES` map in `web/app.js`, plus the curves settings panel
  replacing the "no engine yet" notice. Same everyday/advanced split.
- **Measure the bundle with `--features photo` before deciding anything.** The
  baseline is 152 KB, and the rule was: under ~50 KB of growth keep one bundle,
  over that build two `pkg/` directories with `wasm-pack --features`. The gate
  exists and is verified to compile without `photo`, so this is now purely a
  measurement.
- **Real progress.** The photo path is loops over rows and regions, so it can
  report actual progress through a callback instead of the indeterminate bar the
  sprite path uses. At half a second per image that matters more than it did at
  150 ms.

## 2. Phase 4: the fitters

What the IR guarantees, and what a fitter may therefore assume:

- Every boundary appears **once**, as a half-edge with a region on each side. Fit
  it, and both faces reference the same geometry — one forward, one reversed. There
  is no seam to hide and no stacking needed. This is the thing VTracer cannot do.
- `points` is the **dense** polyline, one point per lattice corner.
- A chain that closes on itself **repeats its first point at the end**. That is the
  signal to fit it as periodic instead of leaving two loose ends.
- A chain's endpoints are nodes, shared with every chain continuing past them.

### `polygon`

Ramer–Douglas–Peucker per half-edge, tolerance in pixels. RDP keeps the endpoints
fixed, which is exactly what is needed: the nodes must not move, or the chains
meeting there come apart. Nearly free, and the right answer for flat-shaded art.

### `spline`

Corner detection over a turning-angle window, tangent estimation, least-squares
cubic fit with recursive subdivision at the worst point (Schneider). Roughly
potrace's pipeline, and the part with real mathematics.

Three details that are easy to miss:

- **`path_data` only knows `h`/`v`** (`svg.rs`) and needs a curve-aware sibling
  emitting `C`. `path_precision` and coordinate quantisation belong here too.
- **`shape-rendering="crispEdges"` is hardcoded in the `<svg>` element**
  (`svg.rs`). It is right for a pixel staircase on integer coordinates and wrong
  for curves — it will alias every Bézier. It has to become conditional on the fit.
- **Tangent continuity across a node is not guaranteed.** Each chain is fitted
  independently, so two chains meeting at a node agree on the point but not on the
  direction. For a three-region junction that is correct: it is a real corner.
  Where it will show is a node that is geometrically smooth. The fix, if it turns
  out to matter, is to estimate the tangent at shared nodes once and hand it to
  both chains as a constraint. Worth knowing before it looks like a bug.

And the reason the whole two-axis shape exists: **`grid` + `spline`** — vectorising
a sprite with smooth curves *after* recovering the grid, so the curves follow the
art pixels instead of the upscaling artefacts. Nothing else does that.

## 3. Photo goldens

The pixel-art safety net is eleven snapshots; the photo path has 60 unit tests and
no snapshot. It cannot use the corpus, which is not in the repo, so it needs a
synthetic fixture built in-test the way `tests/golden.rs` builds its sprite. A
gradient plus a couple of flat blocks plus a deliberate one-pixel line would
exercise the palette, the banding, the speck filter and the thin-feature risk at
once.

Add it once the CLI can emit a photo conversion, so the snapshot covers the real
entry point rather than an internal call.

## 4. Leftovers not tied to a phase

- **Corpus in CI.** Still not reachable from a clean clone; `REQUIRE_CORPUS=1` is
  wired and waiting. Release asset, fixtures repo, or LFS.
- **`.d.ts` guard.** Nothing catches a breaking change to the JS API, and photo
  mode is about to add a second result type to it. Build the wasm in CI and diff
  `web/pkg/img2svg.d.ts` against a committed copy.
- **`checker.rs` has no golden from a clean clone**, same corpus reason.
- **Homebrew Rust shadows rustup locally** — fix on the machine, not in the repo.
- **The look-at-it loop is manual.** Writing an SVG from a test, screenshotting it
  with headless Chrome and cropping it with the `image` crate is how three separate
  findings turned up. It is worth a small script rather than being rebuilt from
  scratch each time — but it must stay out of CI: it needs Chrome and it answers a
  question no assertion can.

## What keeps being true

Four things have now recurred often enough to be worth stating as rules rather
than as anecdotes:

- **The eleven pixel-art snapshots do not move.** They survived the rename, phase
  1, the colour work, the half-edge convention change, and the whole photo
  pipeline. Nothing above has any business touching them.
- **Fewer colours is not fewer regions.** It held for the tolerance sweep (9,409
  regions at 0.08 against 11,690 at 0.15) and again for `max_colors` (16 colours
  giving *more* regions than 31 did). Any future knob that reduces the palette
  should be measured on region count, not assumed.
- **Measure, then look.** The region counts said specks were 68% of the problem;
  only looking at a magnified conversion showed that a second kind of speck — the
  one-pixel fringe band — was invisible to an area threshold. Numbers found the
  first half, the eye found the second.
- **Three of the plan's specifics were wrong, and all three were caught by trying
  to justify them in a comment.** The Oklab rationale, the truncating quantiser,
  and the "background needs a tolerance" premise. Writing down *why* is what
  surfaced them, so keep doing that.
