# Illustration, and the working scale

Started 2026-08-11 22h08. Planned; nothing implemented yet.

Continues [2026-08-11_12h45](2026-08-11_12h45.off-the-pixel-lattice.md), whose 4a
and 4c shipped and whose 4b was withdrawn. The feedback that opens this session
says the results are still far from what is wanted:

> el cover ahora queda muy pixelado, y el Sonic no veo los degradados
>
> sobre la transformación pixelated, funciona muy bien, no cambiaría nada
>
> sobre la transformación a curves, mejor llamémosla **illustration**, es más
> coherente con lo que busco
>
> lo ideal es reinterpretar la imagen como una ilustración típica de web, con
> pocas curvas y pocos colores, manteniendo la precisión en los bordes

And, on how to expose the fix:

> si el problema para simplificar es la escala, podemos ofrecer un `range` al
> usuario, buscando antes el valor óptimo

So: `pixelart` is done and is not to be touched. The other mode is renamed and
its target is restated — **a web illustration**, not a faithful re-encoding. Four
properties now, the three from last session plus the one that was implicit:

- **few colours**
- **few curves** (few anchors, and smooth where the drawing is smooth)
- **crisp edges** where the source has a crisp edge
- **smooth shading** where the source shades

## What the two images do today

Defaults, current `main`:

| | colours | paths | anchors | SVG |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `cover.jpg` (300×300) | 20 | 501 | 11,820 | 77.3 KB |
| `Sonic1.png` (1800×2823) | 21 | 6,895 | 306,675 | 1,870 KB |

Looked at both, and each complaint is a different defect:

**The cover is pixelated in two ways at once.** Its ink lines are *broken* — the
spectacle rims come out as dashes, the mouth as three separate lumps — and every
contour wobbles by a pixel. The broken lines are `min_thickness: 1`, which is
documented to eat everything one pixel wide and does exactly that: at 300 px this
drawing's strokes *are* one to two pixels wide. `--min-thickness 0` puts them
back and brings the antialias fringes with them (22 colours, 817 paths, 128 KB),
which is the trade that constant exists to make. The wobble is the fit tolerance
being pinned at 0.5 by last session's patch, below the rung where anything
straightens.

**Sonic has no shading left, it has grain.** The source is an airbrushed scan: the
body is a smooth blue ramp with soft transitions. The output is flat blue with a
speckled coastline where each band boundary should be — the transition zones come
out as thousands of mottled specks. Zoomed to 1:1 on the chest, the source is a
soft tan-to-blue gradient and the output is two hard bands separated by a fractal
edge. None of the existing knobs fix it: `--min-color-share 0` gives 86 colours
and *more* grain, `--smoothing 6` leaves it, `--gradient-step 0.1` flattens to 14
colours and still speckles the seams.

## The diagnosis: one bug, two directions

Every simplification constant in this pipeline is an **absolute pixel count** —
`filter_speckle: 4` px², `min_thickness: 1` px, fit tolerance `0.5` px, and the
tracing lattice itself is the source's pixel grid. Whether those numbers mean
"simplify" or "destroy" depends entirely on how many pixels the source spends on
a feature, and the two images sit on opposite sides of it:

- the **cover** spends ~2 px on a stroke, so the constants are the size of the
  drawing and the lattice is too coarse to hold a curve;
- **Sonic** spends ~200 px on a feature and ~2 px on its grain, so the constants
  are the size of the *noise* and simplify nothing.

Two experiments, both with the pipeline unchanged, only the input rescaled.

**The cover, upscaled 3× to 900, with the constants kept at their physical
meaning** (`--min-thickness 3 --filter-speckle 36 --fit-tolerance 1.5`):

| | colours | paths | anchors | SVG |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| now, at 300 | 20 | 501 | 11,820 | 77.3 KB |
| at 900 | **16** | **318** | 8,497 | **59.8 KB** |
| at 900, `--tolerance 0.06` | **14** | **240** | | 62.1 KB |

Fewer colours, 37% fewer paths, 23% smaller, and the drawing is *right*: the rims
are continuous, the hair reads as strands, the faces are clean.

**Sonic, downscaled to 900 on the long side, defaults untouched:**

| | colours | paths | anchors | SVG |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| now, at 1800×2823 | 21 | 6,895 | 306,675 | 1,870 KB |
| at 574×900 | 18 | **376** | 34,447 | **198 KB** |
| at 383×600 | 18 | 264 | | 110 KB |

18× fewer paths and 9× smaller, the grain gone, and the shading legible again.

Note where both landed: **900 px on the long side**, one by multiplying and one
by dividing. That is not a coincidence to build a constant out of — 900 is where
these two images' features happen to be a comfortable number of pixels across —
but it is the shape of the answer.

Two things that will not do this job, and both were tried:

- **A pre-blur is not it.** A 1.5 px Gaussian on Sonic does take 6,871 regions to
  1,961 and 1,870 KB to 981 KB, so grain is real and removing it pays. But on the
  cover a 0.6 px blur makes things *worse* (131 KB), because there the noise and
  the drawing are the same size. Denoising cannot be the primitive; it is a
  consequence of choosing a scale.
- **`smoothing` is not it.** It regularises *labels*, one crown per pass, and a
  soft transition eight pixels wide straddles the threshold across all eight. Six
  passes leave the coastline where it was.

This also explains, in retrospect, every measurement in the last session: why
upscaling the cover 4× fixed it, why sub-pixel contours helped the cover and did
nothing for Sonic, why `filter_speckle: 4` "does not scale with the canvas", and
why the tolerance ladder had no rung to stand on. **The rungs are at 0.5 and
0.707 absolute pixels; they stop biting as soon as the lattice is fine compared
to the tolerance.** Last session tried to find a tolerance below the rungs. The
fix is to move the lattice, not to dodge the rungs.

## The plan

### 1 — The working scale, and one range that sets it

The mode gets a **working resolution**: the image is resampled before it is
segmented, and the SVG's viewBox keeps the source's dimensions, so nothing
downstream and nothing user-facing changes shape.

The knob is not the resolution, though — it is the thing the user actually cares
about, which is *how much survives*. One number, in source-relative units:

> **`detail`**: the size of the smallest feature that stays, as a fraction of the
> image. Everything smaller is merged away.

From it, two derivations:

- the **physical constants** — speckle area, thin-region threshold, palette
  tolerance floor, fit tolerance — are all defined as multiples of that one
  length, so they mean the same thing on a 300 px cover and on a 5 Mpx scan;
- the **working resolution** is chosen so that the length lands on about **3
  working pixels**: fine enough that the fit tolerance sits well above the 0.707
  rung and an arc keeps its curvature, coarse enough that grain below it is
  averaged out by the resampling itself rather than by a separate filter.

Upscale and downscale both fall out of that single rule, which is what makes it
worth having as a rule.

Interface, as asked: a **`range` in the panel** with the auto value preselected,
and `--detail` on the CLI defaulting to auto. The existing knobs stay where they
are, under Advanced, because they are what one reaches for when the single range
is not enough — but they stop being the first thing anyone has to understand.

**Finding the optimum.** Two candidates, and the plan is to build the cheap one
first and only escalate if it is not good enough:

- *From the image's own edges.* Where two regions meet, `subpixel.rs` already
  solves for how much of each colour is in a boundary pixel. The **width of that
  mixture zone** is a direct measurement of feature scale: a hard edge mixes over
  one pixel, a soft airbrushed transition over eight, and grain mixes everywhere.
  A percentile of that distribution is an estimate of the smallest length the
  source actually resolves, and it costs one pass.
- *A ladder with a knee.* Segment a proxy at three or four scales and take the
  knee of paths-against-colour-error. Honest, and several times the cost.

Open: whether the estimate is stable on a drawing that mixes both — the cover has
hard poster edges *and* JPEG ringing — and what the clamp on the resample factor
should be, since a 4× upscale of a 5 Mpx image is not something to do by accident.

### 2 — Ink is not fringe

`min_thickness: 1` cannot stay as it is: it is the difference between the cover
having lines and not having them, and neither value of it is right. What
separates the two cases is not thickness but **colour**, and the test is one the
codebase already computes:

> A thin region is an antialias fringe when its colour lies on the segment
> between the colours of the two regions it separates — it is a *mixture* of
> them. A stroke does not: black ink between skin and a yellow ground is nowhere
> near the mixtures of skin and yellow.

Merge the mixtures, keep the strokes. `subpixel.rs` already projects a pixel onto
the segment between two palette colours; this is the same projection, asked of a
region instead of a pixel, and it is the honest version of a constant that is
currently a guess.

Open: a fringe between three regions at a corner, and a stroke whose own colour
*is* between its neighbours' — a mid-grey line between black and white, which is
rare in the artwork this mode is for but is exactly where the test fails.

### 3 — Few curves: smooth the contour, then fit

Even with clean labels and a fine lattice, the boundary of a shaded band wobbles
at pixel scale, and last session established that no chord tolerance can remove
that wobble without also removing small arcs: RDP cannot tell a 45° staircase
from the edge of a circle, because locally they are the same points.

A **corner detector** can, and that is the piece that is missing. Turning
accumulated over a window is small and distributed along an arc, and concentrated
at a corner. So: find the corners of each ring, pin them, and fit the smooth runs
between them with curves whose error is measured against the *filtered* contour
rather than against every pixel step. Corners stay sharp — that is "precisión en
los bordes" — straight runs stay straight, and arcs come out as one or two cubics
instead of forty segments.

This is 4b from the last session, which was withdrawn for good reason: measured
then, `spline` cost 8,751 anchors against `polygon`'s 7,647, because it was
fitting curves to a staircase. It becomes worth doing once 1 and 3's filtering
give it a curve to fit, and the check is the same measurement re-taken.

### 4 — Shading: soft edges want gradients, hard edges do not

`ramp.rs` merges a stack of bands when one `<linearGradient>` beats a flat colour
at reproducing them. On Sonic at the working scale it fires 9 times and the
shading is legible, so part of this is already fixed by step 1. What is left is
the two-band case: a soft transition that the palette splits in two is a gradient
the current criterion refuses, because it requires three distinct colours — and
that requirement exists for a good reason, since any two adjacent regions can be
explained by some shallow ramp.

The mixture-zone width from step 1 is what breaks the tie, and it is a property
of the *source*, not of the palette:

> A boundary whose mixture zone is one pixel wide is an edge the source drew
> hard, and it stays hard. A boundary that mixes over many pixels is a soft
> transition, and a gradient across it is the honest rendering.

That measurement is what lets a two-band ramp be accepted without opening the
door to every pair of neighbours, and it is the same number that keeps poster
edges crisp. Last in the order because it depends on 1 for the scale and on 3 for
the seam geometry, and because it is the only piece the two images do not already
force.

### 5 — The rename

`photo` → `illustration`, everywhere it is user-facing, with the old names kept
as aliases so nothing that exists breaks:

| | now | after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CLI | `img2svg photo` | `img2svg illustration`, alias `photo` |
| page tab | "Foto" | "Ilustración", `#illustration`, hash aliases `photo` and `curves` |
| panel | `PhotoPanel.jsx` | `IllustrationPanel.jsx` |
| constants | `PHOTO_POLYGON_TOLERANCE`, `PHOTO_FIT_TOLERANCE` | `ILLUSTRATION_*` |
| docs | `docs/curves.md` | `docs/illustration.md` |
| tests | `tests/photo.rs`, `foto-*.svg` snapshots | `tests/illustration.rs`, `ilustracion-*.svg` |
| library | `ClusterOptions`, `Config::cluster` | unchanged |

The library keeps `cluster`, because there the name says what the segmentation
*is* — a clustering, as against a grid — and that is still true. What changes is
the name of the **mode**, which is a claim about what the output is for, and
"illustration" is that claim. The docs get the same treatment: `curves.md` was
named after a fitter and describes a mode.

Rename first or last is a real choice. Proposal: **first**, in its own commit,
because it touches many files shallowly and doing it after the algorithm work
would bury one in the other.

## What this is not

Not a fidelity project, and step 1 makes that sharper than before: resampling
throws information away on purpose. The check is the four properties at the top,
and specifically that **the cover's lines stay whole and its lenses stay round**
at the same time as the path count falls. If a step lands and the drawing is
merely smaller, it has failed.

Nor is it a licence to touch `pixelart`, which the feedback explicitly says is
right. Anything shared between the two modes — the fitters, the boundary IR —
changes only in ways `pixelart`'s golden snapshots survive unchanged.

## Order and what to measure

1. rename (no behaviour change; snapshots renamed, contents identical)
2. working scale + `detail` range + auto estimate → re-measure both images
3. ink vs fringe → the cover's lines, with `min_thickness` gone as a guess
4. contour smoothing + curves as the default → anchors, re-taking 4b's numbers
5. soft-edge gradients → Sonic's body, and the flag test still refusing

Each step re-runs the two images in `examples/results-to-improve/` and the
synthetic ramp, and reports colours, paths, anchors and bytes. Steps 2 and 4 are
the ones that can regress the other's numbers, so both get measured after each.

## 2026-08-11 22h30 — The baseline is chosen, and "noise" gets four names

Feedback on the experiments above, before any of them was built:

> los mejores resultados, sin ser ideales son:
> - `.tmp/look/sonic-n600.svg` y `sonic-n900.svg` (no hay casi diferencia)
> - `.tmp/look/cover-mt0-tol07.svg`
>
> al menos son los más fieles a la imagen original
>
> faltaría simplificar el ruido que tienen

Two things follow, and both change the plan.

### The acceptance test now has a floor, and it is fidelity

Those three files are the **baseline**: whatever gets built has to be at least as
faithful as them and quieter. That rules out one thing immediately — the
`up3-a`/`up3-c` variants that got the best numbers (16 colours, 318 paths, 59.8
KB) were run with `--min-thickness 3`, and *that* is what was not chosen, because
scaling the thickness threshold with the canvas erodes ink just as reliably at 900
as at 300. **The working scale must not carry `min_thickness` up with it.**

Re-run at 900 keeping every stroke (`--min-thickness 0 --filter-speckle 36
--tolerance 0.07`): 11 colours, 628 paths, 150.7 KB. Compared at 3× on the same
lens against the chosen `cover-mt0-tol07`, it is strictly better geometry at the
same fidelity — the mouth is one continuous stroke instead of five chunks, the
chin is a smooth arc, the `b` of "blur" has clean sides, the green counter of the
`o` is round instead of octagonal. And it is 33 KB bigger, all of it halo. So the
scale step stays, and its own test is that comparison.

### `sonic-n600` ≈ `sonic-n900` is a measurement, not a shrug

"No hay casi diferencia" between 383×600 and 574×900 says the working scale has a
**plateau**, and that is what makes step 1's auto value practical: the estimate
does not have to be sharp, it has to land on the plateau. It also says to take the
cheap end of it — 110 KB against 198 KB for the same drawing.

### The four kinds of noise, and why they are four

"Simplificar el ruido" is the whole remaining job, so it is worth being precise
about what the noise *is*. Zoomed at 3–8× on both baselines, it is four different
things living in three different domains, and no single knob touches more than one:

1. **Halo** — colour domain. Antialiasing between two colours produces a band of
   mixtures, and the palette gives it an entry: a warm brown outlining every dark
   stroke on the cover, a pink one around the white `blur`. This is what
   `min_thickness` was aimed at and it is why killing it broke the ink. Step 2's
   mixture test is the cure, and it is the single highest-value piece for the
   cover — the halo is the most visible defect left in the chosen file.
2. **Wobble** — geometry domain. ±1 px steps along a contour that should be
   smooth. Step 3 (filter the ring, pin the corners, then fit).
3. **Coastline** — geometry domain, but caused by softness. Where a transition is
   wide, the threshold crossing wanders and the seam between two bands comes out
   fractal. It is Sonic's remaining defect, on the chest and around the quills.
   Steps 1 and 4: the resampling averages what is finer than the working scale,
   and where the mixture zone is genuinely wide the answer is a gradient, not a
   seam.
4. **Split ink** — colour domain, and new to this list. One stroke comes out as
   two tones side by side, near-black and dark grey, because a 2 px stroke never
   reaches full ink: the *whole stroke* is a mixture, so a thin one is lighter
   than a thick one and the palette separates them. Visible on the cover's
   eyebrows against its hair. Candidate cure: collapse the dark low-chroma
   entries into one ink after the palette is built. That is a "few colours" move
   of exactly the kind this mode wants, and it is measured before it ships,
   because it is also the kind of move that flattens a drawing that *meant* to
   have two greys.

Which reorders the work. The scale step still goes first among the algorithm
changes — it is what the other three are measured on top of — but **the halo is
the next one and not the last**, and the plan's step 2 and step 3 swap places:

1. rename to `illustration`
2. working scale + `detail` range, `min_thickness` explicitly *not* scaled
3. halo: the mixture test replaces `min_thickness` as a guess
4. split ink: collapse the ink entries, measured against flattening
5. wobble: corner-preserving contour filter, then curves as the default
6. coastline: soft-edge gradients

Baselines to beat, copied out of `.tmp/look/` so they survive the scratch
directory: `cover-mt0-tol07.svg` (14 colours, 657 paths, 117.3 KB) and
`sonic-n600.svg` (18 colours, 264 paths, 109.5 KB).
